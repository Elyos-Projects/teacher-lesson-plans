Open Educational Resources (OER) are abundant but rarely *teachable as-is*. A teacher who finds an openly-licensed reading passage, a simulation, or a problem set still has to do the hard, unpaid work: write learning objectives, align to the standards their district mandates, sequence a class period, build formative checks, differentiate for mixed-ability learners, prepare materials lists, and assemble it all into something they can actually deliver tomorrow morning. That assembly labour is the gap between "free content exists" and "a child got a good lesson." It is also exactly the gap that commercial marketplaces (e.g. pay-per-download lesson sites) monetize.

**Definition of shipped:** green + the plan **adopted by a real teacher/partner** (taught, or accepted by a repository for reuse) with adoption evidence recorded in the outcome ledger — **or**, if no adoption channel yet exists, published openly with the blocker explicitly surfaced and `verifiedNeed` left
